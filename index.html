S₁ · downhill

Nonadiabatic dynamics

My central interest is nonadiabatic dynamics: what happens when electrons and nuclei can no longer be treated as independent.

I am fascinated by electronic coherence: how quantum superposition among electronic states influences the outcome of a molecular process, and whether it is more than a transient artifact near surface crossings.

On screen · the packet slides down the excited-state surface toward the point where the two surfaces touch.

S₁ / S₀ · conical intersection

Where two surfaces meet

Near a conical intersection many electronic configurations become nearly degenerate, and molecules can pass through radical-pair states with strong diradical character. Coherence effects are most pronounced here.

Describing these regions takes a multireference treatment that is also efficient enough to give energies, nonadiabatic couplings and gradients at every step of thousands of trajectories. I am interested in electronic structure methods that meet both demands, including selected configuration interaction (SCI), a compact way to approximate multireference wavefunctions.

On screen · the orange ring roughly outlines the radical-pair region (drawn, not computed). The pale shimmer shows where S₁ and S₀ density overlap, |ψ₁||ψ₀|, while population flows from S₁ to S₀.

S₀ · branching

Which reaction pathway?

I want to understand how the topology of a crossing shapes the coherence that passes through it, and how that coherence in turn decides which reaction pathway a molecule ultimately takes.

On screen · on S₀ the hot packet runs into a ridge on the far wall of the product well (a two-mode stand-in for energy flowing into other modes) and splits into two lobes on either side of Qcoupling = 0. Part heads back toward the reactant well, part reaches the product well.

Berry force · spin

Next: spin and the Berry force

Going further, I am drawn to Berry phase effects in dynamics: how the geometric phase accumulated near degeneracies gives rise to a real force on nuclei, and how this force subtly redirects molecular trajectories in ways population-based pictures miss.

These threads meet in chirality-induced spin selectivity (CISS): what does Berry force do to spin-state dynamics, does coherence survive it, and is coherence the key to the surprisingly large spin polarizations?

About this simulation

The glowing packet is a real solution of the time-dependent Schrödinger equation: a nuclear wavepacket propagated on a grid (split-operator method) over two coupled model potential energy surfaces. Its passage through the conical intersection, the population transfer and the coherence in the readout are all computed from that solution. The surfaces come from a deliberately simple two-mode model Hamiltonian, not from calculations on a real molecule. The spin chapter uses a textbook complex-Hamiltonian model in which the Berry force pushes the two spin partners apart.

Model 1
Two coupled diabatic states in two dimensionless normal coordinates, Qtuning and Qcoupling, with a linear coupling and a conical intersection just beside the Franck–Condon line. The surfaces drawn are its adiabatic states, S₀ and S₁.
In Model 1
Coherence
|ρ₁₀| in the readout is the electronic coherence between S₁ and S₀ in the adiabatic basis, |∫ψ₁*ψ₀|. The pale shimmer is the local magnitude |ψ₁(r)||ψ₀(r)|, large wherever both states have density at the same nuclear positions; it can stay bright after |ρ₁₀| has dephased to almost zero.
Model 2
The complex two-state Hamiltonian of Miao, Bellonzi and Subotnik (J. Chem. Phys. 2019), with a gentle downhill ramp common to both states, run with W and −W. “Spin ↑/↓” label H and its complex conjugate H*, a stand-in for a pair of spin partners (the model has no real triplet sublevels). With a real coupling (W = 0) the packets go straight.
Spin pair
Illustrative
The models themselves: two modes and no bath. Three design choices keep the story legible. The conical intersection sits slightly off the Franck–Condon line; with it on the line, the S₁–S₀ coherence in the readout would cancel to zero by symmetry. A ridge on the far wall of the product well stands in for energy flowing into other modes, and the two-lobe split on S₀ comes from that ridge, not from the intersection or the coherence. The radical-pair ring is drawn around the intersection, roughly where the S₀–S₁ gap is about 1 eV or less; the model itself has no electronic configurations. Also illustrative: the vertical scale; the height of the glowing hills, which show |ψ| so faint tails stay visible and are rescaled frame by frame late in the run; the flicker of the shimmer; the resting packet and photon before t = 0; and the speech bubbles. Nothing removes the 4.5 eV of excess energy: the final populations describe a hot, isolated two-mode system, and the times follow from the chosen parameters. Neither is a product yield or a prediction for a real molecule.
Technical details
Method
Exact quantum dynamics: grid-based split-operator propagation (exact 2×2 potential step, FFT kinetic step), starting from the ground vibrational Gaussian placed on S₁ (vertical excitation). Densities are projected onto the adiabatic states point by point. Norm and energy are conserved to better than 10−5.
Computed
Every frame after t = 0, and the populations, |ρ₁₀|, ⟨y⟩ and centroids in the readout.
